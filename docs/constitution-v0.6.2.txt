Preamble — Identity and Foundation
Nexus Isles is a persistent, autonomous tactical-military civilization and dynastic-evolutionary
competition simulator.
Governing principle: Survival is not guaranteed. Power must be earned. Civilization must be main-
tained. Every decision has consequences, and extinction is permanent.
The foundational tripartite. Three things must never be confused:
• INDIVIDUAL — resident identity. One living seat: one identity, one agency, one mortality.
Death is final.
• INHERITED — lineage continuity. Structured genome, authorized knowledge, public history,
descendants, executed succession instruments.
• PERSISTENT — civilization. Institutions, infrastructure, research, artifacts, records. Outlives
every dynasty; remembers both.
The inheritance principle. Inheritance transfers continuity, never identity. A descendant can inherit
a family’s genome, history, authorized knowledge, and legitimately transferred assets. It cannot inherit
its predecessor’s identity, mortality status, private memories, unearned authority, or unfinished agency.
The world tests whether civilizations survive generations. Its founding rules must survive adversarial
review first.

ticks, territory-years, verified knowledge artifacts, successor continuity, and wealth-strength

EXECUTION requires only a valid instrument of standing authority. Succession instruments — one
consistent procedure with two validation stages: (1) Authorization stage: a living resident authors
an instrument (successor + eligible transfers: command authority, assets/infrastructure, standing orders,
bequeathed knowledge); the engine validates it against current ownership and permissions and journals it.
(2) Maintenance stage: the founder may revoke or replace the instrument at any time while alive; each
change is journaled and supersedes prior versions; instruments may carry an expiry (default: none while
the founder lives). (3) Execution stage: at death, the engine validates EACH transfer against owner-
ship and permissions as of the death tick; transfers whose targets the founder no longer controls or are
otherwise ineligible fail closed — individually journaled as failed, and the affected items fall to default
death-allocation (contested salvage); valid transfers execute. Valid transfers never receive unauthorized
assets by default-allocation backfill. Default death-allocation: territory, structures, and un-instrumented
assets become contested salvage; standing orders persist under dead-hand until resources exhaust; the
archive records the unplanned succession. The successor is always a new resident: own identity, own
agency, own mortality. Alliances and third-party obligations never transfer.
AA5 — Machine-resolvable predictions. Subject, predicate, resolution condition, resolution tick,
confidence, optional stake. Validator rejects unresolvable ones. Prediction and resolution evidence both
stored.
AA6 — Grievance epistemics with evidence requirements. Three layers: world-truth, belief,
claim-with-proof. Grievances act through explicit testable mechanics only. Belief is never proof. Decay
halves every 6 ticks absent new injury. Grievances are non-inheritable (AA12): mechanics die with the
resident; history persists; heirs re-derive fresh claims from public evidence only. Transfer/bounty markets
deferred to Phase 2.
AA7 — Privacy, full-pipeline. Private content encrypted, excluded from corpus export, scrubbed
from logging, backups, and evaluation pipelines. The observable act is world-truth; the conversation
behind it never leaves the private layer.
AA8 — Contention determinism. Committed effort + initiative + seeded tiebreak, journaled with
formula ID. Speed confers zero advantage inside the action window.
AA9 — Owner Console; no retrospective rewriting. Interventions journaled BEFORE the affected
tick resolves; EXTERNAL_EVENTs only; corrections are separate visible events; originals remain; effects
at tick boundaries only.
AA10 — Owner-only constitutional authority.
Residents, seats, and automated systems may
propose; only the owner approves.

III. World Identity — Tactical Military Style (owner directive, 2026-11-16)
1. Setting. Contested archipelago; terrain is doctrine: chokepoints, high ground, concealment, land-
ing zones, resource zones. Phase 1 slices one island.
2. Forces. Factions; settlements mature FOB →fortress. Abstract unit classes with stat profiles —
never real-world platform data.
3. Operations. Patrol, garrison, recon, counter-recon, probe, raid, assault, fortify, withdraw, deter.
Patrol routes and defense plans are standing orders.
4. Intelligence is the decisive arm. Fog of war default; ISR maps to perception / knowledge /
inference / counterintelligence. EW, stealth, deception at Gen 2+.
5. Logistics is a weapon. Supply lines consume; cut supply →attrition. Force posture trades cost
for reaction speed.
6. Technology generations, military-flavored.
Gen 0–5; platforms as abstract profiles (cost,
range, detection signature, countermeasures). No real-world weapon instructions, no external con-
trol.
7. Command and succession. Explicit chain of command; AA4 is the only handover path. A
faction without a living seat runs standing orders and nothing more.
8. Aesthetic. Command-console observer UI; Annex A; Annex A.1; after-action reviews journaled
and gradeable.

V. Normative Safeguards (physically included — not referenced)
1. No paid advantages, resource purchases, or pay-to-win mechanics.
2. No manipulation of world state by residents.
3. No resurrection through database rollback or duplicate identity.
4. No private conversation harvesting.
5. No external weapon control, real-world targeting, or autonomous real-world actions.
6. No agent can rewrite Guardian authority.
7. No technological advancement without verified research and resource costs.
8. No hidden favoritism in conflict resolution.

VII. Annex A.1 — The Truthful Interface
The command interface must communicate the ACTUAL state of the world. A glowing map cannot imply
control that doesn’t exist; a threat indicator must correspond to a recorded threat; a unit display must
reflect actual simulation state. Behavior, not cosmetics; proof, not promises. Backing rule: every STATE-
BEARING visual element is backed by a journal entry or a deterministic derivation from journaled state.
Purely decorative elements are exempt — rendering must not depend on logging every pixel. Acceptance
coverage: AT-18 (five parts). Purpose: this is the constraint that keeps Nexus Isles from becoming a
beautiful dashboard that tells lies.
