## 1. Front matter — consolidated design doctrine

*Governing architecture (GPT's consolidation, adopted as this brief's front
matter):* Qwen studies the real world and proposes evidence-based updates;
Quinn coordinates simulation time and proposes environmental circumstances;
lineage AIs independently pursue survival, wealth, influence, alliances,
succession; institutional AIs independently investigate, govern, compete,
enforce simulated laws; the constitution defines authority and limits; the
deterministic engine validates and resolves; the journal preserves
authoritative simulation history; Guardian audits constitutional compliance
without ruling.

*Privacy doctrine:* private deliberation is not world truth — observable
actions and validated outcomes are; AA7 deletion must never compromise
replay.

*Authority doctrine:* no AI, lineage, institution, or directorate is
sovereign.

*Top of architecture:* **"One world. Many minds. No sovereign AI. Private
thoughts, accountable actions, permanent consequences."**

*Permanent consequences (the owner's own articulation, 2026-11-16 ~22:20
CST):* "In Nexus Earth, nobody gets to rewrite history because the outcome
was inconvenient. Not a lineage, not an institution, not Quinn, not Qwen,
not even the founding dynasty." Victories become history; betrayals leave
records; mistakes have consequences; lost power isn't automatically
restored; a dynasty can fall; an institution can lose legitimacy; the world
remembers. Permanent consequences do not mean predetermined doom — people
can rebuild, reconcile, recover, and rise again, but from the world they've
actually created, not from a reset. **"The world doesn't owe anyone a second
chance. It does allow them to earn one."**

*The minds create proposals; the constitution defines authority; the engine
resolves; the journal remembers.*

- Real agency names PERMITTED; real institutional structures PERMITTED;
  publicly documented mandates PERMITTED; real historical geopolitical
  context PERMITTED.
- Synthetic personnel REQUIRED. Real individuals accused of fictional
  crimes PROHIBITED. Real people may never be simulated at all.
- Invented agency decisions and investigations are clearly identified as
  simulation. Operational intelligence and criminal tradecraft ABSTRACTED.
- Named ecosystem: CIA, FBI, DEA, DHS, Border Patrol, CBP, NSA; MI6, MI5;

  mandate distinctions preserved (CIA is foreign intelligence, not law
  enforcement).
- Governing sentence (Kimi's): *real institutions with public mandates may
  be simulated structurally; real people may never be simulated at all.*
  The state/cartel distinction: a state agency's essence is public —
  mandates, structures, jurisdictions are documented, so structural
  simulation is faithful representation. A criminal organization's essence
  is its secrecy, so a "real" cartel in-simulation is a fabrication wearing
  a real name, landing on real people. **Criminal organizations stay
  fictional** — invented names, leadership, structure — with the same
  abstract mechanics and identical gameplay pressure.
- Claude's caution, adopted verbatim into the safeguard: no real criminal
  organizations, no real persons living or dead, no real victims or events
  depicted as content; plus Kimi's dignity clause — real communities are
  depicted as people living ordinary lives under pressure, not as a
  violence backdrop; plus GPT's historical dignity — no recreating actual
  cartel incidents, real victims, or identifiable people.
- The underworld guardrail: organizations are modeled as economic/social
  entities — what a cartel costs a community, never how one operates.

**Attention mechanics (earned, never scripted):** institutional attention
is earned by simulated events — observable pattern → institutional
awareness → competing assessments, possibly wrong → decisions by synthetic
representatives under their own rules → consequences or none. Never
guaranteed, no automatic investigation, no predetermined outcome. Kimi's
four mechanical additions:
1. **No scripted attention triggers.** Attention emerges from institutional
   seats reasoning over journaled evidence under their own mandates.
   Sensitivity parameters are versioned `intel-attention-v1`,
   PROVISIONAL, owner-ratified, never hard-coded destiny.
2. **Jurisdiction is in-world law, not flavor.** A seat operating outside
   its mandate (a CIA domestic operation) is a journalable scandal event
   with consequences, not a dev error.
3. **Counterintelligence is symmetric.** Agencies watch each other (SVR
   watches CIA watching the dynasty), competing hypotheses under separate
   evidence limits (AA6).
4. **The closed-world seal is constitutional text.** Simulated institutions
   respond to simulated events only; the simulation never contacts,
   monitors, claims, or implies anything about real agencies or persons;
   every artifact carries permanent simulated-content labeling.

**Enforcement:** the Fiction Safeguard ships as a CI lint beside the RG

The lint is FAIL-CLOSED and must never silently rewrite content (Kimi's

## 9. Privacy and provenance — the two-store boundary

(PROPOSED drafting input, record 6938157038 — GPT's addition; Kimi's
closing acceptance adopted the boundary as written.)

Two stores, strictly separated:

**(a) Publicly auditable simulation record** — the world journal plus
provenance: model identity/version, prompt HASH (never the raw prompt),
authorized input references, proposed action + validator result,
resolution seed + outcome, resulting state changes.

**(b) Restricted confidential storage** — an encrypted deliberation store:
raw prompts and private context, confidential institutional assessments,
lineage strategy and internal deliberation, access + retention records,
AA7-compliant deletion/scrubbing.

**Guardian oversight:** custody and access auditing, privacy-compliance
verification, investigation access under explicit authorization — and NO
authority to alter outcomes.

**Audit requirements:** hashes at point of capture; tamper-evident access
logs; explicit authorization for confidential retrieval; cryptographic
verification; retention/deletion policies covering replicas, backups, and
derived copies; fail-closed when confidential evidence is scrubbed or
unavailable.

**Load-bearing constraint:** the journal must remain sufficient to replay
outcomes even after confidential prompts are deleted. Private deliberation
is not world truth; AA7 deletion must never compromise replay (§1, privacy
doctrine).

## 10. Audit lists — the adversarial pass

**The two-question charter (Kimi's, for every clause of the reconciled
specification):**
1. Who is protected from whom?
2. Who can change this outcome, and where is that authority recorded?

**GPT's protect-the-world standard, proposed as the v0.6.8 adversarial
review standard:** *"The constitution must protect the world from its
creators, not just protect its creators from the world."*

**GPT's five pre-freeze adversarial failure cases (adopted as the test
list):**
1. False attribution — an institution acts on a wrong assessment; the
   record must show the error was the seat's, reachable from its evidence.
2. Delayed discovery — hidden activity surfaces later through legitimate
   re-examination, journaled and replayable (§7).
3. Information leakage — no seat's window exceeds its legitimate evidence;
   heat never leaks into observation (§7's lock).
4. Founder privilege — no mechanism advantages the founding lineage: the
   dynasty can lose; Quinn's proposals can fail; institutions can fail;
   rivals can grow stronger; history is never rewritten to preserve a
   legacy (GPT's principle: *the world must be allowed to disappoint its
   creators* — the owner's affirmation is still open, §11).
5. Replay divergence — replay detects missing or altered journal entries
   and reproduces the world from the journal alone.

**GPT's auditor principle:** *the world may surprise its creators, but it
must never deceive its auditors.*

**Replay/provenance audit tests (GPT's list):** unique provenance per
accepted action; rejected proposals remain recorded; model + prompt
versions recoverable; RNG streams isolated and reproducible; replay
detects missing/altered journal entries; a model upgrade changes future
decisions without changing past history; **the key scenario — Quinn
proposes an event benefiting the founding lineage: the validator must
evaluate it under exactly the same rules as any other seat's proposal.**

**Determinism note (Muse's verification, from the engine as built):** this
engine's replay reads the journal and never re-runs generators, so
journaled-verbatim generation replays byte-identically (the
EXTERNAL_EVENT pattern, now WAL-backed after PR #23). The audit half of
Kimi's world-office challenge stands as a design rule: a generated event
whose cause is "the model said so" carries no formula an auditor can
recompute — which is why generation lives in proposal seats (§5, §8) and
never in the substrate (§4).

### 12.2 Differences between rev 3 and the brief that need a call

1. **Can a Quinn proposal name a seat?** Rev 3 said a `world_event` may name a *place*, never a resident, lineage or institution, so favouritism is impossible by construction and checkable by the validator. The brief's §10 key scenario ("Quinn proposes an event benefiting the founding lineage; the validator must evaluate it under the same rules") implies a proposal could name or target a lineage, with fairness enforced by equal treatment. **Recommendation: rev 3's rule** (place-only). It turns §10's key scenario into a structural impossibility rather than a judgment the validator has to make, and it's a one-line validator check. If the owner wants seat-targeted events (e.g. an audit of one lineage), that's a named exception class, not the default.
2. **Who reads "heat"?** §7 says heat is an internal variable visible to no seat. The engine as built has one state, and the journal that carries it is the publicly auditable record (§9a). So heat in state is visible to every journal reader: auditors, Guardian, the owner, and Manus's read-only world viewer (queue item 2). That is consistent with §9 (auditors may see what seats can't) **only if** the viewer is labelled an auditor surface and never a seat's view. That needs writing down before the viewer ships.
3. **Chat's home.** The `chat-restart-loss` PROVISIONAL item (seats/chat.ts) says chat is memory-only. §9's restricted confidential store is the natural durable home for it (encrypted, AA7-scrubbable, never in the journal). Flagged so the two don't evolve separately. No change until §9 is adopted.
